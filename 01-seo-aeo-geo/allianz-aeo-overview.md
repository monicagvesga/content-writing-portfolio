## The challenge

Ranking #1 in classic organic results is getting harder — and increasingly less
relevant, because more and more searches are answered *inside* Google's AI Overview
before the user ever scrolls to the blue links. The real question for 2025+ content is:
**is your content good enough for the AI to quote it?**

## What I did

I wrote a structured, genuinely useful article on common retirement-planning mistakes,
deliberately formatted for extractability:

- Clear, self-contained answers under each subheading (so a single section can stand
  alone as a citable snippet).
- A scannable "mistake → why it matters → what to do" pattern that AI models can lift
  cleanly into a generated answer.
- Plain, authoritative language that signals expertise without fluff.

## The result

My Allianz articles have earned both kinds of win. As of June 2026, the article on
why a car fails the ITV (vehicle inspection) is **cited as a source in Google's AI
Overview** for that query, and also ranks as the **top organic result** for it. A
separate article on real-estate annuities holds **#1 organic**. An earlier piece on
retirement-planning mistakes also held an AI Overview citation; those citations rotate
month to month, so the exact mix shifts over time.

Screenshots of the AI Overview citation and the organic rankings are in
[`/assets`](../assets).

## Why this matters for a developer-first / AI-native company

AEO/GEO is no longer theoretical for me — I have shipped content that AI search engines
actually quote. As discovery shifts from rankings to AI answers, that's the skill that
keeps a brand findable.
